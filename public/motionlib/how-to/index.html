How to use these references

Short, clean motion references for video tools, drawing and storyboarding. Two kinds of clips live here: original performances by one gray stand-in figure, and public-domain film clips with their provenance shown. Nothing here needs an account — find a clip, pick a camera, download, paste it into your work.

1. Motion-transfer tools (e.g. Kling Motion Control)

Download the master MP4 from a performance page and upload it as the motion reference with your character image. Tips last checked 2026-11-16 — third-party limits change, so check the tool's own docs too:

  • The reference needs the head, shoulders and torso visible. The master videos are framed that way; if a tool wants a tighter or wider feel, try another camera treatment of the same performance.
  • Match your character image's framing to the reference where the tool offers an orientation mode.
  • Clips are padded with first/last-frame holds to at least 3.2 seconds, the minimum such tools accept. Each page notes where the action itself runs inside the padded clip.
  • Documented Kling Motion Control input at last check: 3–30 s (3–10 s in match-image-orientation mode), MP4 or MOV, up to 100 MB, short edge at least 340 px, one character in frame.

2. Camera treatments

Original performances are rendered through a small fixed set of camera treatments — Front, Push-in, Arc, Profile, Low. The performance never changes; only the camera does. Use the CAMERA buttons on a performance page to preview a treatment, then download that exact clip. There is no free camera and nothing to pose — that's deliberate: every clip in the library is the same figure, the same performance, ready to use.

3. Drawing and storyboards

Use the BEATS buttons on a performance page to jump to the key moments, pause the video on the frame you want, and draw from it — or download the GIF and step through it in your own tools. The gray figure is a stand-in on purpose: the pose and expression are the content, with no costume, likeness or set dressing in the way.

4. Public-domain film clips

Clips marked PD FILM are cut from public-domain films. Each page names the film, its year, and why it is public domain, so you can use the clip with the same confidence as the originals. Cut points are chosen so one performance carries the moment — an expression, a chase, a reaction.
